Still Operational
Chapter 2: The Broken Link
Draft 1.4.1

At four forty-eight in the morning, Andrew discovered that Darius had prepared enough food to survive a very small siege.

The evidence occupied the common-room table in eight paper-wrapped parcels. Darius stood beside them with a pencil and a look of concentration usually reserved for recipes he was inventing.

“You're not taking all of that into combat,” Andrew said.

“Of course not,” Darius replied. “Some of it is for the journey.”

Nico appeared in the doorway, fastening the collar of his uniform with one hand and holding a piece of toast with the other. His curls had been crushed on one side by a night's sleep and remained rebelliously upright on the other.

Nico stopped beside the table and looked at the small metal thermos. “Darius, is that coffee?”

Darius lifted its lid. “Real coffee. Mara traded a favor for a little tin yesterday. I made enough for eight small cups, if nobody gets greedy.”

“Real coffee before a mission?” Nico said. “Now I'm concerned.”

Andrew accepted the cup Darius handed him. “You were concerned when we got new socks.”

“The socks were suspiciously comfortable,” Nico replied.

Darius poured Nico's share. “Please appreciate what Mara had to give up for those beans.”

Nico lifted his cup toward Darius. “I appreciate every single one. Especially the ones keeping me awake.”

Mara entered with a folded route card and a mug of her own. Her dark-chestnut hair was pulled into a neat ponytail, and her boots still carried dried mud from yesterday's field inspection. She paused when she saw Nico holding up his coffee.

“You can thank me by getting to the gate on time,” Mara said. “Northern corridor remains open. Gate control expects us at zero five twenty.”

“Morning to you too,” Andrew said.

“Morning, Andrew.” Mara laid the route card on the table. “We have thirty minutes.”

Nico lowered his cup. “She even times her generosity.”

“The local detour report came in at three,” Mara said, unfolding the map. “The bridge north of Hamburg is closed to heavy traffic. I marked the western bypass as our primary route.”

Andrew followed the new line past the closed bridge and checked the revised arrival time. Mara had left a narrow space beside it for his signature.

“You've got the amendment ready to transmit?” Andrew asked.

“Ready,” Mara answered. “Just waiting for you to sign it.”

She slid the pencil toward him before he had to ask.

Andrew signed the card. “Western bypass approved. Send it to gate control.”

“Gut.” Mara took the card back. The new route was already queued on her tablet.

Nico sipped his coffee. “Wonderful. An entire crisis resolved before breakfast. Mara, when did you last sleep?”

“I've been awake precisely as long as necessary,” Mara replied.

“That's what I tell Yelena,” Andrew said.

“Which is how I know it's a lie,” Yelena said from the doorway.

Yelena came in carrying her medical case and an open notebook, her ash-brown braid over one shoulder. She took one look at Nico and opened the notebook to a fresh page.

“Nico, how many hours did you sleep?” she asked.

Nico set his cup down. “Are we discussing personal matters before breakfast?”

“Your fitness for duty isn't personal,” Yelena said.

“Five hours,” Nico answered.

“Four and a half,” Andrew corrected, remembering when Nico had finally stopped talking the night before.

Nico glared at him. “Traitor.”

“Better than yesterday.” Yelena wrote it down while Nico tried to read the entry upside down.

Juno arrived from the hangar with a battered inspection board tucked under his arm. He had grease on one wrist and the expression of someone who'd been awake long enough to make breakfast feel like a late meal.

“Is this mine?” Juno asked, taking the parcel Darius held out.

“Yes,” Darius said. “Eat it while it's warm.”

“Brilliant,” Juno said.

Mara glanced at Juno's inspection board. “How's the fleet?”

“Walk-arounds done on all eight,” Juno said. “Six cleared. Two waiting on readings.”

Darius set a cup beside the inspection board, within reach of Juno's clean hand.

Andrew nodded toward the hangar. “What still needs attention?”

“Your steering needs a loaded turn. Nico's standby coolant probe is lagging,” Juno said. “No clearance until I've checked both.”

Sera came in studying a tablet. “Six degrees low at idle. The independent reading is normal.”

“Connector first,” Juno said. “Then compare them under load.”

“Gate control expects us in twenty minutes,” Mara reminded him.

Juno checked the clock. “Enough time, if nobody needs a running commentary.”

“Finish your food,” Andrew told him. “Then finish the checks.”

Juno raised his half-eaten roll in acknowledgment. “Yes, commander.”

Akari pushed into the room with her camera against her chest and stopped in the middle of fastening her jacket.

“The hangar lights are still on,” she told Andrew. “If I stand by the courtyard wall, I can get the whole row as you come out. All eight, with the gate behind you.”

“If you can do it without holding anyone up,” Andrew said.

“Ten seconds. Less, if Nico stays where I put him.”

Nico lowered his toast. “I was standing perfectly still yesterday. Then you moved the camera, and somehow my foot became the problem.”

Akari was already backing toward the door. “Courtyard wall, Andrew. Don't let him choose the angle.”

Darius distributed the remaining parcels. Andrew's held a cheese roll and something sweet he couldn't identify. Nico tried to negotiate for Juno's until the mechanic, without looking up from his board, took a large bite.

A few minutes later, Andrew led the others across the courtyard. The depot was barely awake. Light showed through the kitchen window, rainwater filled the cracks between the paving stones, and someone was trying to start a stubborn utility truck near the gate.

The hangar doors stood open. Eight XR-17s crouched in two rows, their white armor patched and streaked with mud. Every one had the same compact six-legged chassis, rotating main turret, twin upright missile pods, nose-mounted rotary cannon and circular rear reactor. Their pilots had found smaller ways to tell them apart.

Juno disappeared between DJ's front legs with a tool in his hand. Above him, a crooked sound wave ran under the large letters DJ. Someone had stuck MIC STILL ON beneath the paint; Nico had answered with a smaller label of his own: THAT'S THE POINT.

“Hold the light on the connector,” Juno said.

Nico crouched beside him. “I could hold it better if you hadn't put your whole head in the way.”

“There. Keep it there.”

Sera watched the readings from the boarding step of OWL, one boot beside a tiny pixelated cat. The pale-blue owl painted beneath her callsign was hardly larger. Andrew had once watched Akari try to persuade her to make both bigger. Sera had pointed out that she could see them perfectly well.

Mara called Andrew over as she climbed into CLOCKWORK. Its precise white lettering sat above a little clock face set to five twenty. She checked the time on her wrist before turning the navigation display toward him.

“Gate control has the amendment. The file is on everyone's system.”

Andrew checked the new route against the card he'd signed. “Good. Keep the old crossing marked closed.”

“Already done.” Mara reached past him to close the map. “We can still make our slot.”

Across the aisle, Darius lifted his food bag through BISCUIT's hatch. A painted wheat sprig curled beneath the cream lettering, beside a biscuit with one bite missing. Nico had given him the name after Darius brought fresh rolls to a training exercise; eventually Darius had stopped trying to answer to anything else.

“Water?” Yelena asked him, securing her medical case in the next cockpit.

“Behind the seat. Enough to share.” Darius patted the bag into place. “Food too.”

Yelena nodded and climbed into ICYHOT. Pale frost edged the first half of its name, copper-red paint the second; a tiny blue flower curled toward a red flame beneath it. As she pulled the hatch down to check its seal, Andrew caught sight of the note fixed inside: Drink water. Stay alive.

Akari returned from the courtyard and ducked under SHUTTER's lavender viewfinder markings. Before climbing in, she smoothed a loose corner of tape inside the canopy. Three small photographs sat above her instruments: Darius cooking, Nico asleep, and an inexplicable pair of boots.

“Still keeping the floor?” Andrew asked.

“Look at the edge. That's Sera's hand. She was trying to steal the camera.” Akari pressed the tape flat once more. “I like that one.”

She settled into the seat and tucked the camera into its padded compartment.

“Readings agree,” Sera called from Owl. “Idle and loaded.”

Juno withdrew from beneath DJ, checked the closed connector housing and added his signature to Nico's startup log. “Cleared. Don't pull that lead when you stow your kit.”

Nico looked into the compartment. “I can move the bag.”

“Please do.”

Juno carried his board toward RATCHET. Weathered orange stripes crossed its side armor; beneath them, a white spanner sat under lettering that sloped slightly uphill. He stopped on the step to check the other pilots' startup records before boarding his own XR-17.

Andrew reached SHEPHERD at the far end of the aisle. His plain white letters and uneven crook were beginning to chip. Akari had offered to straighten the emblem; he'd refused. He'd painted it himself, after Nico spent a whole evening calling him Shepherd for rounding up squadmates who'd wandered away.

He ran a hand along the old scuff beneath the cockpit, then climbed aboard.

“Ratchet to Shepherd,” Juno said over the maintenance channel. “Steering response is the last check. Take it through the marked test lane; I'll watch the load readings.”

Andrew secured the harness, closed the canopy and brought the reactor online. Under Juno's direction, he moved Shepherd through a short turn in the service square, then returned to the hangar apron.

“Owl to Ratchet. Response within limits,” Sera confirmed over the maintenance channel.

Juno checked Sera's readout against his own. “Ratchet to Rook element. Eight mechs inspected. Eight cleared to move.”

Juno passed the signed inspection board down to the duty technician. Andrew rechecked his canopy seal and watched seven friendly markers join his own on the squad net.

“Shepherd to Rook element,” Andrew said on the squad net. “Communications and launch check. Respond by callsign.”

“Clockwork to Shepherd. Navigation loaded. Systems green,” Mara reported.

“Shutter to Shepherd. Recon sensors green,” Akari reported.

“Owl to Shepherd. Sensor net synchronized,” Sera reported.

“Ratchet to Shepherd. All eight inspections signed off. Systems green,” Juno reported.

“Biscuit to Shepherd. Weapons secured. Systems green,” Darius reported.

“IcyHot to Shepherd. Medical kit secured. Systems green,” Yelena reported.

“DJ to Shepherd. Primary and fallback channels checked. Systems green,” Nico reported.

The final marker turned green. Eight pilots ready.

“Shepherd to Rook element. All stations green,” Andrew said. “Weapons on safe through inhabited corridors. Clockwork leads navigation. Maintain intervals and report faults immediately. Destination: Neumünster forward depot.”

Andrew shifted the controls. His XR-17 lifted from its resting posture, all six legs extending together until the cockpit rose above the hangar floor. He felt the weight transfer through the chassis, the faint vibration as the reactor took the load.

Akari's harness and canopy confirmations appeared beside Shutter's marker. Ratchet moved into place at the rear of the column.

Andrew called the depot gate on the traffic frequency. “Shepherd to Gate Control. Eight XR-17s ready to depart on the cleared northern route. Request movement.”

“Gate Control to Shepherd. Route clear. Proceed through the gate in sequence,” came the reply.

The gate slid aside.

“Shepherd to Rook element. Move out,” Andrew ordered.

The eight mechs walked through under their own power, turned north and accelerated when the last civilian building was behind them.

At first the mechs moved with the careful precision required by the roads near Celle. Their clawed feet landed on the paved shoulders beside the military lane, each step measured to avoid broken drainage covers and civilian cables. The six-legged gait was almost gentle at this speed. The pilots could have been escorting a parade.

The houses fell behind them.

Beyond the last checkpoint, the land opened into broad fields, low industrial buildings and service roads marked for military traffic. Mara took them onto a hardened strip running along the edge of an abandoned freight route.

“Clockwork to Rook element. Open ground in two hundred meters. Accelerate in formation. Maintain intervals,” Mara said.

The speed indicator climbed.

Andrew felt the XR-17 lengthen its stride. Its front legs reached forward, found ground and released almost before he registered the contact. The middle pair carried the weight through the turn of the gait; the rear pair drove the body onward. At speed, the movement became fluid enough that the cockpit scarcely pitched.

Forty miles an hour. Sixty. Eighty.

Juno let out a brief laugh on the internal channel, then went quiet again to watch the fleet temperatures.

The speed touched ninety-seven miles an hour and settled. Their XR-17s could sustain a hundred on good ground, but Mara kept room in the formation for turning and stopping. A flock of birds lifted from a ditch; Akari adjusted her course around it without losing her interval.

They slowed before the first occupied village. A faded school sign stood beside a row of houses with patched windows. On the square, traders had arranged jars, vegetables and homemade soap beneath an awning. A line of people waited outside a ration office. The eight XR-17s passed at a crawl.

A boy in a yellow coat waved both arms.

Akari raised one of her mech's front legs for a brief wave.

“Shutter, marked lane,” Mara reminded her.

Akari brought the foot down inside the painted boundary.

The boy laughed and kept waving. Andrew noticed his mother pull him farther from the road, her eyes on the mechs rather than the market stalls.

At the next checkpoint, a column of civilian vehicles was waiting under a temporary roof of camouflage netting. Some had luggage tied to their roofs. Others carried mattresses, bicycles or plants in plastic buckets.

An elderly man sat on the edge of a truck bed, holding a cage covered with a towel. Whatever lived inside shifted when the XR-17s passed.

The checkpoint officer appeared on Andrew's forward display, helmet camera catching a tired face and a sleepless pair of eyes.

The checkpoint officer studied Andrew's identification on the video feed. “Walker detachment? Confirm eight XR-17s.”

“Shepherd to Checkpoint Control. Acting commander Walker, eight XR-17s northbound to Neumünster sector. Request route clearance,” Andrew replied.

“Checkpoint Control to Shepherd. Clearance granted. Keep west at the next junction. Engineers still working the eastern span,” the officer replied.

“Copy. Any change to the northern threat report?” Andrew asked.

“Artillery contact farther north. No confirmed breach on your corridor. Monitor the route net,” the checkpoint officer replied.

Andrew glanced toward the civilian column waiting behind the barriers. “And the people at your gate?”

The officer followed his gaze. “Heading south when the transport queue allows. Still have a few coming north for duty.”

Andrew acknowledged the report. Families might be evacuating, but the people keeping the hospitals and roads operating still had to travel toward the front.

The officer waved them through, and the barrier rose.

When the squad was clear, Mara switched them back to the military corridor. They accelerated again, though not as fast as before. A maintenance convoy occupied one lane, and beyond it crews were reinforcing a line of concrete barriers. Their bulldozer had lost a track and stood crooked beside the road.

“Those people have been waiting since before dawn,” Darius said on the internal channel.

“Probably longer,” Yelena replied.

Nico wondered aloud what was in the covered cage. The old man steadied it against the truck's vibration, keeping one hand on the towel even after the mechs had passed. Andrew found himself watching that hand until the truck disappeared from his rear display.

North of Hamburg, the clouds lowered and the landscape changed. Defensive markers appeared beside the road, then concrete firing positions, then embankments built from earth and broken stone. The population was thinner here, though no less busy. Mechanics worked beneath vehicles. Uniformed volunteers carried cable drums. A group of children helped stack empty crates outside a clinic while a tired teacher tried to keep them away from the road.

On a rise to the east, Andrew could see an artillery battery adjusting its barrels.

The radio carried a request for replacement ammunition, followed by a reply too faint to make out.

“That's not our sector,” Mara said.

“I know,” Andrew replied.

A few moments later the ground gave a distant, hollow thud. The artillery line had fired.

Nico reduced the volume of the general traffic channel. “They've been busy.”

Andrew asked Nico to keep the depot channel open. The forward depot came into view shortly after eight, almost three hours after they'd left Celle. Checkpoints and slower sections had taken more of the morning than the stretches of open ground.

It occupied the grounds of a former distribution center, surrounded by hurriedly built walls and an assortment of buildings that had not been designed to withstand a battle. One warehouse had lost half its roof. Workers had enclosed the loading docks with sandbags. A row of civilian vans waited behind concrete barriers.

There were fewer mechs here than Andrew expected.

The depot sentry cleared the gate for their eight XR-17s, then directed them to a hardened inspection pad. Andrew brought his mech down into a low waiting posture while the other seven took positions around the square.

Nico started to lower DJ across the medical entrance. Mara pointed out the sign, and he moved aside before anyone had to ask twice.

Andrew opened his canopy. Cool air washed across his face, carrying the smell of damp concrete and engine exhaust. He climbed down from the mech and approached the orange-jacketed woman waiting at the edge of the pad.

“Acting commander Walker?” the woman asked.

“Andrew Walker,” he said. “Rook detachment. This is Mara Venn, my deputy. Reporting with eight mission-ready XR-17s.”

“Leonie Krüger, communications maintenance lead.” She shook his hand. “Good. You're ahead of the movement window.”

“We've come to get your repair team through,” Andrew replied.

“Good,” Leonie said. “I don't think this station has another afternoon to spare.”

Behind her, two small utility vans stood packed with replacement cable, tools and portable batteries. A young technician was checking a coil of wire, while an older driver studied a greasy map.

Leonie followed Andrew's glance toward the vans. “Mika's our junior technician. Oskar's driving the second van and handles our power gear. Three of us, two vans. We weren't going to risk more people than we needed.”

“What failed at the station?” Andrew asked.

“The primary data line has been dropping out for two days,” Leonie said. “Last night the backup power feed started failing too. We suspect stormwater got into the coupling housing.”

“You haven't confirmed the fault?” Mara asked.

“Not until we open the cabinet,” Leonie replied. “The parts are in the van. The problem is getting there.”

Mara nodded once. “Understood.”

Sergeant Brandt met them at the depot office, acknowledged Andrew's salute and led them to the operations map. The sergeant wore a stained field jacket over his uniform and looked as if he'd come directly from the overnight watch.

“Relay's here,” Brandt said, tapping a mark northeast of the depot. “The eastern bridge is underwater. Engineers closed it last night. West route goes through the old workshop ground and cuts back toward the relay after the drainage culvert.”

Mara leaned over the map before Andrew had finished studying it. She drew a circle around the workshop yard. “We can hold the vans here if the culvert isn't clear. There's room to turn them around.”

Brandt looked toward Andrew rather than the pencil in Mara's hand.

“That's our proposed fallback,” Andrew confirmed. “Mara planned the route. I'll give the move order once reconnaissance checks the far side.”

Brandt nodded at her markings. “Good choice. We used that yard for recovery traffic before the floods.”

“What about West Watch?” Mara asked.

“Two observation posts,” Brandt said. “The northern one evacuated before dawn to support the batteries. The southern post still has eyes on the western field and the workshop approach, but its radio is unreliable without the relay.”

“Can they confirm the far end of the culvert?” Andrew asked.

“Negative,” Brandt said. “They can see the western field and our side of the service road. The culvert exit itself is blind.”

“I'll take Shutter along the western field and clear the exit,” Mara said. “The rest of you bring the vans up once we have it.”

Andrew traced the drainage bank with one finger. “How long would you be out of sight?”

“Two minutes. Less on firm ground.”

“With an unreliable radio and nobody watching the far side.” He tapped the workshop yard beside her pencil. “We bring the vans here first. Shutter observes from the near rise. You stay close enough to support her.”

Mara kept her hand on the pencil. “That leaves us waiting in a position they may already have ranged.”

“And taking two mechs around the bank could leave you cut off. We hold the hardstand until we know what we're moving into.”

Brandt waited beside the map. Mara looked from the rise to the yard, then redrew her approach line.

“Then we'll need someone covering this fence. The buildings hide the drainage ditch.”

“Yelena can cover it from the rear,” Andrew said. “Put it on the formation.”

Mara made the amendment. “Understood.” She left the earlier route visible beneath the new one.

Brandt moved his finger farther north. “Two sectors have been asking for reinforcements since midnight. I can't spare another unit for your approach.”

“Then let's get their radios working,” Leonie said.

Andrew checked the latest patrol times with Brandt while Mara amended the formation. Nico programmed a short-range fallback frequency and coded light signals. Outside, Juno inspected all eight XR-17s for loosened fittings and travel damage; Sera compared the results with the logged temperatures and actuator loads.

Yelena opened Leonie's medical case.

“You have dressings,” Yelena said.

“Yes,” Leonie answered.

“Tourniquets?” Yelena asked.

Leonie pointed to a side pocket.

“Good,” Yelena said. “Keep them where you can reach them.”

Mika, the young technician, watched her repack the case.

“Will we need those?” he asked.

“Hopefully not,” Yelena said.

He didn't look reassured.

She closed the case and handed it back. “That's why we're bringing them.”

When Juno returned with all eight cleared, Andrew checked the escort speed with Leonie. Thirty kilometers an hour would keep the vans together on the service road. Their drivers would stop at the workshop hardstand if the far approach remained unobserved.

Brandt's desk radio crackled. A voice from a northern battery requested confirmation of a firing lane. Brandt answered in clipped German, then switched the set off again.

“Stay on your assigned route,” he told Andrew. “I can't spare another unit if you go looking for a bigger fight.”

“We aren't looking for one,” Andrew assured Brandt.

Brandt glanced at the eight mechs outside. “Glad to hear it.”

Andrew signed the escort sheet, passed the final route confirmation to Mara and climbed back into his XR-17.

The pilots confirmed escort readiness while Nico brought the technicians onto the convoy channel. Two sets of brake lights glowed against the wet loading docks.

“Shepherd to Rook element. Escort formation. Move out,” Andrew ordered.

The two utility vans looked very small between eight XR-17s.

Andrew had seen columns like this before. A few ordinary vehicles carrying something essential, surrounded by mechs worth more than the roads beneath them. The people doing the essential work were rarely the ones with armor.

“Is this your normal escort speed?” Leonie asked over the civilian channel.

“Only when we're trying to impress the passengers,” Nico replied.

“Then you've succeeded,” Leonie said.

Nico let out a quiet laugh. “A rare review I can put in writing.”

At thirty kilometers an hour, Shepherd shortened its stride and kept more feet planted through each weight transfer. Andrew could feel the deliberate placements through his seat. Beside the vans, a misplaced step mattered more than speed.

Andrew and Mara led. Darius and Juno kept to either side of the vans. Sera monitored the eastern drainage channel, Akari screened the western fields, Nico handled communications, and Yelena covered the road behind them.

The road narrowed between fields heavy with rainwater. Ahead, the old eastern crossing had vanished beneath brown floodwater. Only the tops of two markers and the broken edge of the bridge were visible.

Mara marked the submerged crossing closed on the shared map.

The convoy turned west onto her service road.

It passed an abandoned workshop complex built around a broad paved yard. Most of the windows had been boarded over. A sign marked VEHICLE MAINTENANCE leaned against a fence. Someone had drawn a line through the word VEHICLE.

Juno turned his cockpit toward the abandoned workshop's open loading bay as they passed. Nico started to suggest a salvage stop. The unobserved strip appeared on their shared route display, and he left the thought unfinished.

Beyond the workshop yard, the service road curved toward the culvert. The drainage bank blocked the view beyond it for nearly three hundred meters. On Andrew's display, the far side remained an empty strip marked UNOBSERVED.

“Shepherd to Shutter. Report ahead.”

“Fresh soil on the east bank,” Akari said. “Something climbed out. No visual yet.”

“Owl to Shepherd. Cold returns under the road,” Sera added. “The bank blocks identification.”

Andrew asked Nico for West Watch. The first call brought only static. On the second, a voice broke through long enough to say east bank and movement.

“DJ to West Watch One. Repeat contact location.” Nico adjusted the frequency and tried again. He let the silence run, listening for anything beneath it.

Mara placed the cover markers around the workshop yard. This time she left them unassigned.

“Shepherd to Rook element. Hold at the hardstand. Biscuit and Ratchet on the vans; IcyHot, cover the rear fence. Shutter, observe from the near rise. Clockwork, support her.” Andrew switched to the civilian channel. “Leonie, both vans into the yard. Keep the gate clear.”

The first van turned in. Akari took the rise with her chassis low and her turret facing the drainage bank; Mara settled below her, covering the road.

“Movement. Eastern ditch,” Akari said.

“Count?” Andrew asked.

“Still coming up.”

A figure rose behind the concrete edge of the culvert.

At first Andrew saw a young man struggling up from the drainage channel. His short hair lay flat against his forehead. The skin of his face was pale but almost uninjured, and his eyes searched the road with the helpless confusion of someone who had woken in the wrong place.

He opened his mouth. His lips shaped a word Andrew couldn't hear through the cockpit glass.

Then the man hauled himself onto the bank. His shirt had split across the chest, revealing metal beneath the skin. Dark struts rose beside his sternum and vanished into raw tissue at the shoulders. Something moved under his throat as though a cable were pulling the tendons there. His expression stayed frightened even after his limbs drove him forward with brutal, purposeful speed.

Another head appeared beside it.

Then a third.

“Huskframes. Fifteen visible, more behind,” Akari reported.

“Leonie, engines off. Everyone stay inside,” Andrew ordered.

The first Huskframe stumbled off the bank. For two steps it moved like a wounded person, dragging its boots through the mud. Then its posture snapped upright and it ran toward the workshops as fast as its rigid legs would carry it.

Andrew fired a short rotary-cannon burst into its chest. The body buckled backward, the face retaining its bewildered expression as it fell into the ditch.

More Reclaimed came over the bank.

His optics caught a woman with wet hair stuck to her cheek and an elderly man whose lips kept moving. One figure raised a hand toward the mechs. Andrew almost lifted his finger from the trigger; then the wrist bent against its steel brace and the body accelerated straight into Sera's fire.

“Clockwork, west approach. Owl, keep them in the ditch. Everyone else, hold your sectors,” Andrew ordered.

Mara moved to cover the workshop lane. Sera's next burst struck the bank, throwing mud and broken concrete over the figures climbing it. Several fell; others stepped into the gaps.

The sound of impacts beneath the road made Andrew check his footing.

A chunk of concrete lifted beside the culvert.

“Under the road,” Sera warned. “Shepherd, beside your front feet.”

A low shape unfolded from the broken drainage pipe. Its back was a cage of rusted braces threaded with swollen, pale tissue; beneath it hung a man's head, nearly untouched. Water ran through the hair and over his blinking eyes. The creature dragged itself forward on four bent limbs, scraping its knuckles against concrete until the joints stiffened and it accelerated.

“Crawler at your front joint!” Nico called.

It lunged at Andrew's front joint. He lifted the leg, moved sideways and fired downward, driving the Crawler into the open trench.

Another emerged behind it.

Then another.

The repair vans sat less than a hundred meters behind the squad's forward positions.

Darius turned his XR-17 sideways across the workshop entrance. Its six legs braced along the road edge, armored body protecting the vehicles while its turret tracked the drainage gap.

“Biscuit, fence!” Nico called.

A Crawler slipped beneath the broken panel and caught the second van's axle. Its weight dragged the rear wheel sideways. The van jolted against a concrete post; glass burst from the passenger window.

“Something's under us!” Oskar shouted.

Yelena was already moving along the fence. “Oskar, brakes on. Stay inside.”

Darius kept his armored body across the entrance while she struck a broken drainage grate with one heavy foot. The Crawler turned its human face toward the sound but held the axle. Yelena stamped again, closer this time. Metal rang under the van as the grip came loose.

She backed down the empty lane. Only when the Crawler cleared the vehicle did she fire, dropping it beside the trench.

“Clear of the van,” Yelena reported. “Oskar, can you hear me?”

His answer took a moment. “Yes. Yes, we're here.”

At the northern workshop wall, something tall dropped from the broken roofline. Its feet hit the concrete almost silently, long knees compressing beneath a narrow torso whose skin had split around rows of black actuators. A man's face looked out from above the ruin of a collar, his brow still drawn into an ordinary worried frown.

The creature remained absolutely still for one terrible moment. Then it crossed the open ground in a series of violent, almost effortless strides.

“Stalker, north workshops!” Akari called.

Juno's first burst struck the wall as the creature darted behind it. He stayed beside the vans, tracking the building's far corner instead of chasing it.

The Stalker shot out beside Juno's mech. He pivoted sharply, planting his legs on the hardstand while the creature's claws rasped across his outer armor. His rotary cannon caught it in the torso before it could grab a joint.

The Stalker struck the wall and crawled another meter with one arm before stopping.

“Ratchet to Shepherd. Stalker down. Side armor scored; systems normal,” Juno reported.

“Second one. Behind the workshop, toward IcyHot,” Sera said.

Akari left the rise and cut across the hardstand. The second Stalker dropped from the embankment toward Yelena's rear legs. Akari's first burst struck the wall; she lowered her aim as it folded beneath the gunfire and hit it before it reached the road.

“IcyHot, rear clear,” Akari said.

“Thank you.” Yelena kept her gun on the fence opening while Oskar reported that the van's engine would still start.

For a moment, Andrew thought they had contained the attack.

The Huskframes at the culvert were thinning. Sera had the eastern bank covered, Mara held the west approach, and Darius remained between the technicians and anything that could reach them.

Then the remaining Huskframes changed direction.

They withdrew from the road, spreading to either side of the northern exit.

Something struck the culvert wall from beyond the embankment.

The concrete cracked.

A second impact tore away a slab the size of a small car.

“Heavy contact, north exit,” Mara warned.

The Brute shoved through the shattered culvert wall.

It was taller than the workshop gate, with heavy limbs wrapped in overlapping plates of iron and bone. Gray-pink tissue bulged around the joints like muscle forced to grow inside industrial supports. Thick cables entered the flesh at its shoulders and disappeared beneath its ribbed chest; every step pulled against them with a sound like wet leather stretched over gears.

A man's head was fixed above that terrible body. His dark hair was neatly parted despite the mud on his cheeks. Nothing had replaced his eyes or teeth. His mouth moved with the hesitant shape of a man asking a question, while the immense arms tore chunks from the road.

“Brute,” Sera said. “Reinforced chest. Shoulder seams exposed when it reaches.”

Andrew's threat display changed color.

The creature did not turn toward the nearest mech.

It turned toward the workshop yard.

Toward the vans.

“Ratchet, cannon solution?” Andrew asked.

“Biscuit blocks my angle. No shot,” Juno said.

“Hold. Biscuit, keep them covered.”

The Brute advanced down the road, forcing water from the ditch with each step. Its head cleared the workshop gate. Darius could shield the vans from the smaller attackers, but Andrew could see what those arms would do to the buildings around them.

Mara marked the field west of the road. “Firm center. Keep off the drainage edge. We can turn it clear of the vans.”

Beyond the Brute, smaller Reclaimed were withdrawing toward the northern ditch. Andrew could follow their route out of the sector, perhaps find where they'd crossed. His eyes returned to the vans behind Darius. The retreating figures passed beyond the bank without pursuit.

“Clockwork, hold the approach,” Andrew said. “I'll draw it west. Ratchet, take the field entrance.”

“Stay below the northern rise,” Mara replied. “We lose sight of you beyond it.”

Andrew drove into the field, low and fast, and turned Shepherd's nose toward the Brute. His rotary rounds struck its shoulder without slowing it. He fired again, walking the burst across the exposed seam.

The man's head turned. A moment later the body followed, leaving the road and charging toward him.

Andrew slipped sideways and let it pass, then turned back toward its flank. The Brute pivoted faster than he'd expected. A heavy arm swept across his canopy.

He dropped Shepherd low. The blow clipped the left outer plate and drove him hard against the harness. His left rear foot broke through the field's crust into the wet ground beneath. A hydraulic whine rose through the chassis as the trapped leg took the load. The cockpit tilted; a load warning filled the lower corner of his display.

“IcyHot to Shepherd. Damage report,” Yelena called.

“Left outer armor hit. Rear foot sunk; shifting load. No joint warnings.” Andrew tried to draw the leg free. The foot moved, but the field tore away beneath it. He stopped before he dragged the whole chassis lower.

The Brute turned toward him again.

Clockwork shifted along the road, clearing a gatepost from Mara's firing line. Her first burst struck the Brute's upper arm while Andrew was still trying to unload the leg. Her position marker remained on the workshop approach.

“Don't pull against it,” Juno said. “Lower the body. Take the weight on the other five.”

Andrew eased the controls back. The hydraulic whine dropped in pitch as Shepherd settled on its five planted feet. The horizon rose across the canopy. The Brute was close enough for him to see rain running through the man's parted hair.

“Clockwork, hold that angle,” Andrew ordered. “Keep it turned.”

Mara's next burst turned its head. Andrew lifted the trapped foot almost vertically, then drove the other five against the ground. Shepherd lurched free as the Brute's next blow plowed into the place he'd occupied.

“Mobile,” Andrew reported. He had to loosen his grip on the controls. “Clockwork, hold there.”

He moved onto the firm center of the field and fired into the Brute's side. It followed him west, away from Mara and the workshops. Behind it, Juno reached the entrance and stopped short of Andrew's retreat path.

“West Watch receiving,” Nico said. His voice had lost its usual rush. “They have the western field. Nothing beyond your target inside the boundary. Shutter confirms. The northern rise is still blind.”

Andrew kept the Brute below the rise. At the field's southern edge he turned east, bringing Shepherd around toward Juno's side of the engagement. The Brute wheeled after him, exposing its shoulder to Ratchet.

“Clear of your line,” Andrew told Juno. “Take it.”

Orange light ran along Ratchet's cannon.

“Firing,” Juno said.

The discharge struck beneath the shoulder and drove the Brute sideways. Armor buckled around a cavity of cables and pale tissue. It dropped to one knee, one arm buried to the elbow in the churned earth.

Andrew began to turn his own cannon toward the opening.

“It's getting up,” Sera said.

“Still cooling,” Juno answered. “Need another gun.”

Shepherd's left feet began to sink as Andrew lowered it to brace. The chassis leaned under him. He could not hold it steady for the discharge here. The strip of packed track ahead was only a few meters away.

The Brute pulled its arm free.

“Repositioning for a shot,” Andrew said. “Clockwork, keep it turned. Ratchet, clear south.”

Juno backed across the eastern edge of the field. Mara fired from the road again, pulling the Brute's face toward her. It took a step in her direction. Andrew moved onto the track, lowered Shepherd and planted all six feet. This time the chassis held level.

“DJ, western field?” Andrew asked.

“Still clear,” Nico said. “West Watch has you. Shutter has the far boundary.”

Andrew checked the friendly markers. Juno was south of his line; Mara remained behind and to his right, at the road. Beyond the Brute lay the empty western field. Its intact head lifted above the torn chest, the lips moving as though the man were trying to form a name.

“Shepherd firing west. Hold positions.”

Andrew fired.

The energy discharge drove into the Brute's exposed structure. Its arms locked in place, then fell loose. The torso collapsed, the enormous body hitting the ground hard enough to shake the workshop windows.

Andrew kept his turret on it.

The head was still visible above the twisted armor. For a moment it faced the sky, eyes open.

Then the wreckage settled over it.

“Brute down,” Andrew said.

“Two still at the road,” Mara replied.

Darius's rotary cannon fired from the workshop gate. When he reported the last contacts down, Andrew checked the Brute again. It had not moved.

“Shepherd to Rook element. Status check. Report by callsign,” Andrew ordered.

The replies came in order. Mara and Akari were operational; Darius had both vans covered; Yelena was moving to check the technicians. Nico's fallback link held. Andrew entered each confirmation in the log.

“Owl to Shepherd. Mud over the right array. Limited coverage on that side,” Sera reported.

“Ratchet to Shepherd. Side plate scored. All joints responding. Cannon cooling.”

Andrew logged his own armor impact and the overloaded leg, then assigned Akari to cover Sera's blind side until the housing could be cleaned. Eight pilots had answered. He counted the confirmations once more before releasing Yelena to dismount.

Leonie called for a medic as Yelena reached the second van. Andrew left Mara holding the perimeter and climbed down to join them.

The second van's passenger door stood open. Mika sat on the pavement with blood darkening one sleeve. A piece of glass had cut his forearm when the Crawler struck the van.

Mika was trying to stand, using his uninjured arm to push off the pavement. “It's just a scratch,” he insisted.

“Sit down,” Yelena snapped. Mika froze. She'd been so calm during the fight that the sudden sharpness of her voice startled even Andrew. “You have blood running down your hand, and you're worried about getting back to work?”

Mika sat again. Yelena knelt beside him, her movements instantly gentle as she eased the fabric away from his arm. “Let me look,” she said, much more quietly. She cleaned the wound, checked his fingers and wrapped the arm while Leonie held his shoulder.

“You've treated injuries like this before?” Mika asked Yelena.

Yelena did not look up from the dressing. “Yes.”

Mika glanced at the blood on his sleeve. “How many?”

“Enough to know you'll do better if you hold still,” Yelena replied.

Mika held still.

Juno had his inspection tablet out again. Andrew found him moving from one XR-17 to the next, checking scuffed armor, actuator temperatures and the warnings recorded during the fight.

“Give me the short version,” Andrew said.

“Your outer plate's fractured. Foot and joints look sound,” Juno said. “I'll compare the load log before I clear you. Ratchet's side plate is scored; Owl needs her sensor housing cleaned.”

“How long?” Andrew asked.

“Time to do it properly.” Juno was already moving to the next XR-17. “I'll check the van's axle too.”

Andrew looked at the dent beside the van's broken window. “Call me when you have all eight.”

Juno stopped beside the next XR-17 and looked toward the fallen Brute.

For a moment he said nothing.

“Those faces never get easier,” Juno said at last.

Andrew followed his gaze.

A Huskframe lay beside the road, its cheek pressed into the mud. The face belonged to an ordinary-looking woman with a small scar over her eyebrow. Under the torn collar, her spine disappeared into metal supports. Her mouth had stopped moving. Andrew wished he hadn't seen it move earlier.

“No,” Andrew said quietly. “They don't.”

Darius brought water and the breakfast parcel he'd saved. He broke the roll in two and offered the pieces to Leonie and Mika.

Leonie stared at the bread. “After that?”

“Take your time,” Darius said. He put the water beside Mika's good hand and crouched so the young man didn't have to reach for it.

Nico accepted the empty wrapper. “He's been carrying that since before dawn. I'd eat it before he starts asking for a review.”

Mika laughed before he could stop himself, then winced as Yelena secured his bandage.

“There,” Yelena said. “You may laugh, but you're not lifting anything heavy with that arm.”

“That's almost all I do,” Mika replied.

“Then supervise the others,” Yelena said.

Leonie took a bite of the roll. “He'll enjoy supervising us. He already tries.”

Juno finished with the last mech while the technicians packed. All eight were cleared to move; Sera's cleaned array passed its check, and Oskar's axle and wheel still tracked straight. The van would need a new window and bodywork back at the depot.

“Shepherd to Rook element. Shutter and Owl, sweep both approaches. DJ, check the relay on local radio. Clockwork, confirm the culvert is passable,” Andrew ordered.

The squad reported its sectors secure. Mara marked a path between the broken concrete: enough room for the vans to pass one at a time under cover.

The vans pulled out of the hardstand.

The last stretch to the relay took less than ten minutes.

The station stood on a low ridge behind a bent fence, with two squat aerial towers and a concrete equipment building between them. The exterior looked as if it had survived several storms and at least one careless artillery crew.

Nico studied it through his cockpit display.

“After all that, the relay's just a concrete box?” Nico said.

Leonie heard him on the local channel. “If you wanted a beautiful building, you should've joined the museum service.”

Juno laughed.

Inside the equipment building, the air smelled of damp insulation and hot plastic. Several warning lights flashed on a cabinet beside the primary transmitter.

Leonie opened the coupling cabinet. Brown stains marked its floor. A cracked connector sat beneath a layer of corrosion, and the backup power line ran through the same compartment.

“Water got into the lower housing,” she said. “The backup's been carrying the load through this connection.” She pointed with an insulated tool; a flake of corrosion fell away.

“Do you have the part?” Andrew asked.

“Of course. It was in the van. The part was the easy bit,” Leonie replied.

She turned to Mika. “Dry housing, insulated connectors, portable test set. Carefully. And let Oskar carry the battery.”

Mika gave Yelena a wary glance before obeying.

Andrew left the technicians to their work and returned to his mech.

The squad spread around the station, covering the northern approach, the culvert road and the fields beyond the ridge. Their main cannons cooled while the smaller weapons remained ready. In the distance, artillery continued to fire.

Andrew watched the route on his screen.

One section of the communications network had failed, and civilian movement across several checkpoints had stopped with it. It wasn't the greatest threat facing Germany. It might not even make the evening briefing.

But it mattered to the people waiting for someone to tell them they could go home.

“Andrew,” Mara said on their private channel. “About the field.”

He glanced at Clockwork's marker on the road below. “Go ahead.”

“You should have handed the Brute off after the hit.”

“To whom? Juno was getting his cannon into position. Darius had the vans. You were holding the approach.”

“I could have taken it.”

“And left that road uncovered? We still had contacts coming through.” Andrew checked the northern approach before looking back at her marker. “I reported the damage. The joints were responding. I could finish the maneuver.”

“You reported a trapped foot. Then you needed me to pull it off you.”

“Yes. And you did.”

There was a short silence. The local channel carried Leonie asking Mika for a tool.

“Covering you from the road isn't a handover,” Mara said. “By then you were already stuck. You keep putting yourself where the plan has the least room to go wrong.”

Andrew looked at the armor warning he'd acknowledged. He'd had the closest position and the clearest route into the field. He still believed he'd made the right choice. He could also remember the Brute turning while he waited for his foot to come free.

“I won't leave a gap in the escort to protect my own armor,” he said. “But next time we assign a second pilot before I draw something off. A handover point, not just a firing position.”

“That would have helped,” Mara said.

He waited, but she had nothing more to add.

“Put it in the debrief,” Andrew told her. “We'll go through it together.”

“Understood.” Mara returned to the squad channel to confirm her sector was clear.

Leonie gave brisk instructions over the local channel. Mika called out voltage readings, corrected one, then reported a steady result.

Nico reported his short-range set patched into the relay's test circuit. Andrew heard him repeat each reading for Leonie, waiting for her confirmation before he moved to the next.

“Local circuit holds,” he reported. “Ready when you are for long-range.”

“Five minutes,” Leonie said.

“I'll keep the test set on,” Nico replied.

A soft electronic tone sounded in Andrew's cockpit.

Then another.

The northern observation markers, which had been uncertain all morning, began appearing on the map.

“Leonie, I think we have the primary carrier,” Nico said over the local channel.

“Don't celebrate until it passes verification,” Leonie replied.

Static hissed, then a thin voice reached them from farther south.

“...Relay Three, this is Convoy Control. Is anybody receiving?”

“DJ to Convoy Control. Transmitting from Relay Three. Receiving your signal. Stand by for verification,” Nico replied.

“Convoy Control to DJ. Identify your station authority,” the remote operator requested.

Leonie read the relay's authentication sequence while Nico repeated the challenge response and waited for acceptance.

A clean tone sounded.

“DJ to Shepherd. Primary carrier stable. Switching to backup test,” Nico reported.

“Backup stable too,” Leonie said, sounding exhausted and delighted.

Andrew checked the time. The repair was late, but the medical convoy should still be able to make its scheduled passage.

Andrew asked Nico to contact the southern roadblock. Nico switched frequencies and identified the restored relay.

“Southern convoy gate receiving. Send your traffic,” a new voice answered.

“DJ to Southern Convoy Gate. Relay Three communications restored. Local route secure. Official release confirmation to follow,” Nico reported.

There was a brief pause.

“Southern Convoy Gate to DJ. We've been holding civilian traffic since last night. Good to hear you,” the gate operator replied.

“Good to hear you too,” Nico replied.

Leonie came outside with her gloves tucked beneath one arm. For the first time since meeting her, Andrew saw her relax.

“Final diagnostics look good,” she said. “It's working.”

“Properly working?” Andrew asked.

“Don't ruin this for me,” Leonie replied.

“Wouldn't dream of it,” Andrew said.

The first convoy cleared its roadblock a few minutes later.

The medical convoy reported that it had received the restored route information and was preparing to move. The workers at the depot confirmed that their local telephone link had returned.

“Two convoys released,” Nico announced on the crew channel.

“And the maintenance truck at the western gate,” Sera said, reading the clearance messages arriving through the restored relay.

Nico checked the message. “I'm counting that. Three groups moving, and nobody asking us to push a van.”

“Don't offer,” Leonie told him. “Oskar might take you up on it.”

With Mara coordinating the perimeter, Andrew climbed down to meet the technicians. Darius joined him with the water bottle. Leonie drank, then sat on the building's low step for a moment with her gloves in her lap. Andrew gave her the time.

At the edge of the yard, Akari approached the three technicians with her camera.

“Would you mind one photograph?” Akari asked the three technicians. “I'd like to remember who put this relay back together.”

Mika glanced at his bandaged arm. “Even looking like this?”

“Especially like this,” Akari said. “You did the work.”

Leonie glanced at the battered concrete building. “Can we stand somewhere that doesn't make it look as though we repaired a prison?”

“The vans will do,” Akari said. “Oskar, a little closer to Leonie. There's room.”

Andrew watched her arrange them beside the vehicles. Oskar looked awkward, Mika tried to hide his bandage, and Leonie stood with both hands folded as though waiting for an inspection.

“Andrew, can you take it?” Akari asked, holding out the camera. “I want to be in this one.”

Andrew took the camera and checked its display. “Everyone ready?”

“Ready,” Akari said. Leonie nodded, and Mika managed a tired smile.

The camera clicked.

Akari studied the photograph, enlarged it to check their faces, then entered the technicians' names beneath it.

“That's a good one,” she said quietly.

He was about to answer when the restored northern communications channel filled with new traffic.

A battery requested updated coordinates.

A position farther north reported contact beyond its forward obstacles.

Another officer asked for reinforcements at a road junction.

The voices were tired and matter-of-fact. They weren't announcing a victory or a catastrophe. They were trying to keep people informed enough to get through the next hour.

“Clockwork to Shepherd. Depot confirms we're cleared to return once the technicians are packed,” Mara reported. “No new tasking. I've prepared the return route and formation.”

“Shepherd to Clockwork. Good. Hold movement until all three technicians are aboard,” Andrew replied.

Mara acknowledged and held her position. Nico packed the test set and returned to DJ. Andrew and Akari climbed aboard while Darius stowed his bag.

He checked the eight friendly markers on his display. Everyone was at their assigned security position.

“Shepherd to Rook element. Maintain perimeter until the civilians are ready. We'll escort them back to the depot before heading home,” Andrew ordered.

Juno switched to the quieter crew channel. “No racing. Those plates need the workshop.”

“I was going to ask about lunch,” Nico said. “If we stop at the depot long enough. A proper lunch, I mean. Sitting down.”

Darius took a moment to answer. “I'll ask at the depot kitchen. You can sit while I do.”

Andrew glanced at the breakfast wrapper tucked beside his seat and realized he was hungry too.

Akari sent Andrew the image from her camera. The technicians were smiling beside the two vans, tired and dirty and very much alive. Akari stood between Mika and Leonie, her own grin making the photograph look almost like one from a school trip.

“Worth it?” she asked him over a private channel.

Andrew glanced at the three civilians preparing to leave, then at the mechs guarding the ridge. “Yeah,” he said. “Worth it.”

“Good. I'm putting this one up beside yesterday's,” Akari said.

“Mara might complain,” Andrew said.

“She always complains about the frame,” Akari replied.

Andrew laughed. The radio brought another northern request for reinforcements. He marked it for the end-of-mission briefing, then checked his squad's positions once more.

For now, every marker was green. Their job here was done, and they could take the technicians back alive.
