STILL OPERATIONAL
Chapter 3: The Cost of a Good Day
Draft 1.0

Nico had carried the empty lunch tin all the way back from Neumünster without dropping it. He lost control of it six steps from the hangar door.

The lid hit the concrete first. The tin followed with a clatter that brought Juno's head up from beneath Shepherd's flank.

“That better be yours.”

“It is,” Nico said. “Well, technically it belongs to the kitchen up north, but they said to keep it. The handle caught on my radio bag. Andrew, can you get the lid? I've got a cable under my foot and if I move now this becomes much worse.”

Andrew caught the lid before it rolled into the floor drain. There was still a little flour in the rim. They had eaten sitting down at the forward depot, all eight of them, with their helmets under the bench and the three technicians at the next table. Mika had needed help opening a bottle. Oskar had talked through most of lunch, his hands moving whenever he described the van hitting the post.

Leonie had listened, eating slowly. Before they left, she had shaken Andrew's hand with both of hers.

He passed the lid to Nico and took the radio bag so he could untangle himself.

Outside, late-afternoon light lay across the yard. The familiar depot buildings beyond the open doors looked pale and dry after the northern rain. Andrew could hear someone trying to reverse a supply truck into a space that was probably too small. A man beside it gave increasingly personal advice to the driver.

Akari stepped down from Shutter with her camera tucked inside her jacket. She stopped to scrape a thick lump of mud from one boot, inspected the result, and scraped the other.

“I'm printing the photograph before anyone puts more notices on that wall,” she said.

“Wash first,” Yelena told her.

“Hands first. Then photograph. Then the rest of me.”

Yelena seemed willing to accept the compromise. She lifted her own bag onto her shoulder, then looked at Andrew as he tried to do the same with his left arm.

“Harness?” she asked.

“Caught me when it hit the plate.”

She set her bag down. “Show me where.”

He touched the front of his shoulder. Yelena had him move the arm, then pressed carefully through his shirt. The ache stayed shallow and familiar.

“Bruise,” she said. “Tell me if it gets worse. And stop testing it every ten steps.”

Andrew lowered the arm he had already begun to roll again. “Wasn't counting.”

“I was.” She picked up her bag and went after Akari.

Darius carried the abandoned lunch tin toward the kitchen. Nico followed with the radio equipment, telling him that the depot cook had offered a recipe as well, although he had only remembered the first half.

Andrew started toward Shepherd's boarding step.

“Leave your kit,” Juno said. “Need you here a minute.”

He had pushed his goggles into his blond hair. A clean trolley waited beside him, its top covered with folded cloth. With the mech settled on the bay supports, the fractured plate sat just above his shoulder.

“Anything change on the way back?” Andrew asked.

“No. Leg readings stayed good. Taking this off now, checking the mounts, fitting the spare tomorrow. Ratchet's scoring can wait.” Juno tapped the damaged armor with a gloved knuckle. “Need your impact account on the repair sheet.”

From the next bay, Sera raised a hand to get Andrew's attention. She stood beside a diagnostic cable connected beneath Owl's cockpit.

“I'm copying the local recordings before the routine files roll over,” she said. “Including the others.”

“Keep them all. We'll do the debrief after everyone washes.”

“How long?” Juno asked.

Andrew looked at the mud drying on the mechanic's sleeves. “Fifteen minutes.”

Juno held out a pair of work gloves. “Start counting when this is down.”

The plate came away reluctantly.

Juno had supported its weight from the bay hoist before loosening the fasteners, but one edge still caught against its mounting bracket. Andrew steadied it while Juno worked the corner free. The hoist chain clicked. A thin stream of dried grit fell onto the cloth below.

Without the rest of Shepherd around it, the damage looked smaller. Andrew could cover the deepest part of the dent with his hand. The fracture running out from it was harder to dismiss: a pale line through the scuffed finish, open enough at the edge to catch a fingernail.

Juno turned the plate under the work light.

“Can't dress that out.” He traced the line without touching its sharp edge. “I'll mark it for recovery. Mounts look straight. Check those properly in the morning.”

Mara arrived carrying a clipboard and the stock book. She had taken off her field jacket but still wore the muddy boots.

“Stores has one that fits this side,” she said. “It's reserved for us until ten tomorrow.”

“Good.” Andrew kept a hand against the plate while Juno lowered it. “What happens at ten?”

“They release it if we haven't collected it. Another workshop requested the same pattern.” Mara opened the book on a clear corner of the bench. “Ours is damaged. Theirs may be damaged worse. Stores wants the inspection attached.”

“They can have it,” Juno said. “This one's out of service.”

He eased the plate onto the cloth, checked that it lay securely, and slackened the hoist.

Mara ran her finger down the stock entry. Andrew could see an old dispatch stamp beside a newer one, the dates crossed and corrected in different ink.

“Is there another coming?” he asked.

“No confirmed delivery. This has already been transferred twice. It didn't come from a new production batch.” She looked from the stamps to the plate. “We need our stock here to last.”

“Then we'll put in the actual damage and let them compare it.”

“I'm asking you to account for it, Andrew.”

He took his hand off the plate. “That's what I'm doing.”

Juno's wrench stopped against a bolt. For a moment the only noise near them was water moving through the wash station at the far end of the hangar.

Andrew had heard the emphasis on our. He let it pass. They were standing beside a piece of armor he had damaged, and arguing over a pronoun would not get Shepherd repaired.

Mara put the repair sheet on top of the stock book. He read Juno's notes, added the direction of the impact and the fact that he had been turning away from the Brute, then described the foot breaking through the field crust. He included the failed first attempt to pull it free.

“Put the load log with that,” he said.

“Already copied,” Juno answered.

Andrew signed beneath the account.

Mara checked the entry before clipping it to the request. When she opened the folder beneath the stock book, an older allocation sheet came up with it. Twelve names filled the left column. Four had lines through their equipment assignments.

Her thumb stopped along the edge. She smoothed a folded corner that did not need much smoothing, then slid the sheet back beneath the others.

Andrew had seen Matthias's name before it disappeared.

Matthias had ordered him back. He had stayed, and Matthias had come for him. That much did not become less true because Andrew could now stand in a workshop and explain every decision he had made that morning.

He looked again at the fracture in the plate. He still believed drawing the Brute away from the vans had been necessary. The sight of the old sheet made him want to say so, although Mara had not asked.

“Mara. Hold this end,” Juno said.

She set the folder down and took the loose end of the lifting sling.

“Andrew, other side. Don't drag it across the fracture.”

They worked the webbing out together. Mara fed hers under the plate while Andrew lifted the far edge just enough for it to pass. Juno guided the last loop clear and wound the sling around his arm.

“Done,” he said. “No more hands needed. Go wash.”

Mara gathered the forms. Andrew held the side door open for her, and she thanked him as she passed.

By the time they reached the common room, Darius had a pot heating and Nico had found a chair close enough to smell it.

Andrew took the other end of the table for the debrief. Everyone brought something with them: a mug, damp hair, a towel still looped around Juno's neck. Akari had a freshly printed photograph under a clean sheet of paper and refused to let anyone put a drink beside it.

“One change before we're done,” Andrew said. “The field. I reported the armor hit and the trapped foot. Juno's attached the load record. We got me moving, but we didn't have a second pilot set up to take the Brute.”

Mara laid a rough drawing of the workshop road between them.

“Next time we name the second pilot before anyone moves. Here, I take over at the field entrance. Andrew pulls south, Juno keeps his firing line.” She marked the exchange point. “The second pilot starts moving as soon as the first reports a mobility problem.”

Andrew studied the mark. “Explain where you'd bring it.”

She drew a short curve into the western field, keeping below the rise. Her line left room for Shepherd to retreat and would have turned the exposed shoulder toward Juno.

It was a useful route. It also began where Juno had needed to stop.

“Could you have fired from there?” Andrew asked him.

Juno leaned over the map. “Not with both of them crossing my nose. Need a separate patch to brace on. Firm patch.” He drew a square farther south. “Here, if the ground holds. I'd have to check it.”

Darius rested his mug beside his knee. “And Mara leaves the approach to come in.”

“Akari could take it,” Mara said.

Akari shook her head. “I was covering the hardstand. I'd need someone looking behind the workshop before I moved.”

Mara's pencil hovered above the paper.

Andrew could see her reworking the positions. He had done the same arithmetic in the field, and disliked it just as much. Eight pilots looked generous on a roster until each already had something to protect.

“The handover needs its own cover,” he said. “We can't just name Mara and expect everything behind her to stay safe.”

“I can mark that,” she replied.

“And the fallback link?” Andrew asked Nico. “When could we rely on West Watch?”

Nico sat forward. “After you got the foot out. Before that I had them in pieces. Getting a voice once isn't the same as having a link. I didn't have their clear boundary until Akari checked it from our side.”

Andrew turned the drawing toward himself and wrote three short lines at the bottom. Second pilot. Handover position. Cover for the move.

“For the next escort, this goes into the approach plan where the ground gives us a choice. Before someone draws a heavy away, we name all three if there's time. If there isn't, the deputy works the handover while the lead moves. Nico tells us when the fallback is usable. We don't build the plan around having it early.”

Mara read the lines. “The second pilot needs to know they can act without waiting for another request.”

“Agreed. At the point we've assigned, with the cover we've assigned. If that changes, say it.” Andrew looked around the table. “Anything that makes this worse?”

Juno tapped his square. “Still want solid ground.”

“That's in.”

Mara added it beneath Andrew's notes. Her drawing was busier now, several of its first marks crossed out. She kept the pencil until she had made the new route easy to follow.

“I'll copy it into the escort notes,” she said.

Andrew nodded. “Thanks. That's the debrief.”

Darius checked the clock over the sink. “Thirteen minutes.”

Juno took the towel off his neck. “Keep the other two.”

Darius carried the pot to the table on two folded towels. It held potatoes and white beans in a thick sauce, with browned onion worked through it. Andrew had thought the late lunch would keep him full until bed. The smell changed his mind.

“Mustard's there,” Darius said, setting down the serving spoon. “Taste it first, please.”

Nico reached for the mustard.

Darius watched him.

Nico moved his hand to his spoon instead. He took a careful mouthful, considered it with more ceremony than the request required, and reached for the mustard again.

“Now I know,” he said.

“A little,” Darius told him.

Andrew took the pot from Darius before he could begin serving everyone individually. “Sit. I'll pass it.”

Darius hesitated, checked that the stove was off, and sat beside Yelena. She had already put a bowl in his place. Under it was a deck of cards.

He pulled the deck free. “You kept these with you?”

“Since yesterday.”

“Then perhaps I should count them.”

Yelena broke a piece of bread in half. “Count the points properly tonight and you won't need to.”

Darius examined the bent corner of the box, then put it safely beside his mug. “Pairs this time. After we've eaten.”

Andrew passed the pot to Mara. She gave herself a small helping, passed it on, and took a second piece of bread without looking at anyone. The field drawing was folded beneath her elbow. After a few mouthfuls she put it on the windowsill, well away from the bowls.

Nico began explaining why the northern depot cook had given him the tin. Apparently he had been trying to ask about the rolls when another worker handed him a stack of lids, which he had accepted because refusing would have made the conversation more difficult. From there he had somehow become responsible for matching them to their containers.

“So I was helping,” he said, “but every time I got one to fit, there were two more. And the cook kept telling me to push harder. Which, fine, you can say that about a lid, but Juno would have had me out of the workshop if I tried that with anything of his.”

“Bent rims,” Juno said around a mouthful. “Not pressure.”

“I know that now. I didn't know it while I was being given another six. And then he asked whether I'd come for a job.” Nico spread his hands. “At that point I had to admit I was mostly there because Darius wanted to sit down and I hadn't finished asking about the bread.”

“I had already sat down,” Darius said. “You could have joined us.”

Nico looked briefly aggrieved. “I was involved.”

Mara laughed into her cup. It caught Andrew by surprise, a small sound followed by a larger one when Nico turned to her for support.

“You came back with flour on both elbows,” she said. “I thought you'd been leaning against something.”

“I had. A great many things.”

Juno went back for more potatoes. Nobody asked him to explain the rims.

Halfway through the meal, Akari uncovered the new photograph and held it up beside the one already on the wall. The old frame had a worn wooden edge; the new print was smaller, its border crowded by the names she had added at the relay.

“Here,” she said. “Same height. Sera, can you reach the top if I hold it?”

“After you put your bowl down.”

Akari looked at the bowl balanced in her other hand, as though it had only just become an obstacle. She set it beside Andrew and pulled a chair over.

“That's my chair,” he said.

“You're in it. This is the spare.”

“It's my spare.”

Akari gave him the print. “Then make yourself useful while it's away.”

Andrew held it by the white edges. The little image was clearer than it had been on his cockpit display. Oskar's smile had come out crooked. Leonie looked as if the picture had caught her a moment before she decided to relax. Mika was trying to keep his bandaged arm out of the way, leaving a gap that Akari had filled by leaning toward him.

“This one?” Sera asked. She had found a shallow frame in the drawer beneath the noticeboard.

“The corner's loose,” Mara said.

“I can see the corner,” Akari replied.

“You can see it now. Later you'll hear it.”

Andrew pressed the two pieces together and found the little metal tab on the back bent outward. He pushed it flat with the handle of his spoon, wiped the handle, and handed the frame over.

Akari tested the joint. “Fine. You may keep the chair.”

“Generous.”

Sera helped fit the print while Akari kept adjusting it so none of the names disappeared beneath the edge. They tried it twice. The first time clipped the end of Leonie's surname; the second left a narrow strip of backing visible down one side.

“I'll cut a better mount tomorrow,” Akari said. “This is enough for tonight.”

Andrew steadied the chair while she climbed up. Sera stood back beside the table and directed the last small adjustment.

“Left side down. A little. There.”

Akari looked over her shoulder. “Straight?”

“The frame is.” Sera studied it. “The noticeboard isn't.”

Mara got up to look. Andrew expected another correction, but she pressed the lower fastening into the board and moved the pot lid off the chair when Akari stepped down.

“Leave it,” Mara said. “It looks good.”

Akari's smile lingered as she returned to her bowl. Andrew sat with the photograph above his right shoulder and found that his food was still warm.

For a while they ate without much conversation. Juno folded his towel into a pillow against the chair back, then abandoned it when Nico nearly dipped one end in his sauce. Darius watched them, finished his own bowl, and finally took the mustard.

Yelena slid it across without comment.

Sera waited until Andrew had put down his spoon before leaning toward him.

“Could you look at two sections of the recordings after this?” she asked.

“Something missing?”

“No. The order of things. I started checking the contact positions and my first reconstruction didn't fit.”

He glanced at the hall leading to the diagnostic room. “Do you need me now?”

“After,” Sera said. “Nico needs to check the timing with me.”

Nico looked over from the bread basket. “I can do that.”

Andrew nodded, took the last potato from his bowl, and asked Darius which team he was on.

“Mine,” Darius said. “Unless you have forgotten how to follow suit.”

“I remember.”

“We will see,” Yelena said.

Sera had cleared a space at the diagnostic desk by stacking two manuals on the floor. Three mugs still occupied the far end. Andrew recognized his own, abandoned there the previous morning with a pencil across the rim.

He took it to the sink while Nico connected a headset. When Andrew came back, the workshop road filled the monitor.

Sera had paused the recording before the culvert wall broke. The picture was crowded with range marks and the edges of Owl's display. A line of text across the bottom identified the local file.

“Your array?” Andrew asked.

“This was before the housing was fouled. The picture gets worse later. I've marked that separately.”

She moved her chair aside so he could see. Nico sat on the edge of the adjacent desk, one ear covered, comparing two audio tracks.

From the common room came the scrape of chairs and Yelena asking who had spilled something sticky on the score pad. Juno denied it with the speed of someone who knew he was likely to be blamed.

“Just watch the road first,” Sera said.

She started the clip.

Huskframes moved between the broken banks. Andrew remembered the culvert as a mass of competing motion, each new shape replacing the last on his threat display. Here he could watch one of them long enough to see it stop advancing.

It changed course. Another moved away from the road on the opposite side.

He waited for the concrete to break.

The first visible impact came after the smaller forms had already begun to spread. The second tore the slab loose. Sera stopped the recording before the Brute emerged fully.

“I saw them pull back,” Andrew said.

“You put that in the contact log.” She brought up his short entry beside the picture. “I plotted it as a response to the breach. Then I checked the recording.”

He read the entry. Smaller contacts withdrawing; heavy advancing toward protected vehicles. It was accurate as far as it went. It made the two movements sound separate.

“Play it again.”

This time he watched the space the Huskframes left. The road opened between them before the wall broke. He could remember the Brute moving through that gap toward Darius and the vans.

Without his hands on the controls, the pause seemed much longer.

“Could they hear it coming?” Andrew asked.

“Possibly. Feel it through the ground, see something we couldn't.” Sera moved the image back to the intact wall. “We can't see the other side. All I can establish here is that they started moving before the first visible strike.”

Nico took off the headset. “That sequence is one file. I can make the sound clearer, but it won't tell us what they heard.”

Andrew nodded. “What's the second section?”

“Earlier. The vans.” Sera selected another clip. “This one crosses between feeds, so I wanted the timing checked.”

The second van appeared behind the fence. The broken panel lay at an angle against the ground. Andrew knew what would come beneath it and found himself looking at the passenger window instead.

On the recording, the rear wheel jerked sideways. The van struck the post.

Nico lowered the volume before Oskar's shout could fill the room.

Yelena moved along the fence. Her mech's foot came down against the loose grate, lifted, then struck again. The Crawler let go. She backed into the lane, drawing it clear of the van.

Sera paused the image and brought up Akari's view beside it. The angle was higher and farther back. A portion of the workshop blocked the ground near the embankment.

“I started with the times printed on the files,” she said. “They put Akari's movement earlier than it could have been.”

“The displayed time isn't enough,” Nico said. “One feed starts at recording, one at the saved segment. I've lined up the same radio call in both. Then checked it against the next one.” He indicated two narrow marks beneath the pictures. “They're close enough to follow the sequence now. I wouldn't give you an exact gap between events.”

“Show me,” Andrew said.

They played together. Yelena backed down the lane. On the other screen, Akari turned toward the workshop. The first Stalker was on Juno's side, forcing him to pivot on the hardstand. Then Sera's recorded voice warned of the second one moving toward IcyHot.

Andrew leaned forward. Akari's turret moved, hiding part of the embankment. When the view cleared, the second Stalker was already dropping toward Yelena's rear legs.

The clip ended as Akari's next burst hit it.

Sera opened a still of the yard. “The van attack drew Yelena here. The first Stalker kept Juno on this side. The second came in behind her.”

“After she moved,” Andrew said.

“After, as far as the visible approach goes. We don't have its starting position.” Sera left her finger on the blind strip beside the building. “It could have been waiting there. It could have arrived while we were looking at the van.”

Andrew stared at the place where she was pointing. During the fight, he had heard the callouts as separate problems. Crawler at the axle. Stalker near Juno. Another going for Yelena. Each had demanded an answer before he could give much thought to the one before it.

Together on the screen, they looked different.

“You're thinking they opened that lane,” he said.

“I'm thinking they used it.” Sera folded her hands in her lap. “Whether they meant to make it open is the question.”

Nico replayed the short section from Akari's feed. “If I show you only this, it looks like the Stalker saw a back leg and went for it. Add the van, and it looks planned. Same recording.”

Andrew rubbed his jaw. There was a line of dried skin near his chin he had missed when washing.

“And the movement at the culvert makes you less comfortable treating it as chance.”

“Yes,” Sera said. “Two places where their movements fit together. It deserves another set of eyes.”

He asked them to replay the van sequence once more, starting before the Crawler reached it. This time he watched their own formation. Darius stayed across the entrance. Juno held near the vehicles. Yelena moved because someone had to get the thing off the axle without firing through the van.

There was no idle pilot they should simply have sent instead. The gap opened because a sensible response required movement.

Andrew sat back.

“Send both full sections with the shorter clips. Keep the lead-in. Someone needs to be able to disagree with us after seeing the same thing.”

Sera reached for the keyboard. “Possible coordinated pressure on the escort?”

“Yes. With the other explanations you've just given me.”

Nico slid off the desk. “I'll keep the original files separate. The aligned copy can have the timing note on it.”

Andrew went to the doorway. Mara stood at the common-room sink with her sleeves pushed up, rinsing the pot while Darius dried it. He waited for her to finish, then asked her to look at the positions.

She listened without interrupting. At the second still, she pulled the drawing from the debrief out of her pocket and flattened it beside the keyboard.

“The fence is farther out than it looks here,” she said. “This line runs along the workshop wall. The broken section was beyond it.”

Sera adjusted the mark.

“Could you see that strip from the approach?” Andrew asked.

“Only the road end. Not where the second Stalker came from.” Mara bent closer to the monitor. “Akari had the better view, until she moved. There wasn't a clean view behind that building from my position.”

She replayed the smaller Reclaimed clearing the culvert. Her hand remained on the mouse after the clip stopped.

“This should go out tonight.”

“It will,” Andrew said.

“With a warning about moving the rear cover. If they can pull one pilot away with a van, they can do it with any vehicle we have to protect.”

“We can warn people that the opening was used. We can't tell them it was arranged.”

Mara looked at him. “They still need to act before the next escort.”

“I agree. Put the lane exposure in the practical note. And the handover change we're making. Sector can send it to the other units.”

“If it's buried under enough qualifications, nobody will change anything.”

Andrew turned the keyboard toward himself. “Then we make the warning clear.”

He read as he typed. “During the attack on the second van, movement to free the vehicle left a rear approach exposed. A Stalker used that approach. Escort plans should account for cover when a pilot has to move to assist a protected vehicle.” He looked up. “That happened. We can say it plainly.”

Mara read the paragraph on the screen. “Include that the other nearby pilot was also engaged.”

Andrew added it. The practical warning occupied the first paragraph. Possible coordination followed, with the culvert movement and the limits of the recordings beneath it.

He had Sera check the wording on timing before Nico attached the files. Mara moved a label that obscured the broken fence. By the time they were done, the little map showed what each of them had been able to see and the spaces none of their views covered.

Andrew sent it to sector operations with the mission reference and the three of them listed as contributing to the review.

The acknowledgment arrived while Nico was confirming the attachments had transferred. It addressed the commander of Rook element, confirmed receipt, and requested that all original recordings be retained pending review.

Mara read over Andrew's shoulder. She straightened before he finished scrolling, took back her drawing, and folded it precisely along its old crease.

“Received,” Andrew said. “That's enough for tonight.”

“I'll finish the escort amendment,” Mara replied.

“Tomorrow. We have the notes.”

She held his gaze for a moment, then tucked the drawing away. “All right.”

Sera closed the clips. The empty desktop felt unexpectedly easy to look at.

Nico checked the transfer once more, disconnected the headset, and hung it on its hook. From the common room, Darius called Andrew's name.

“Coming,” Andrew answered.

Darius had kept Andrew's place opposite him. Yelena sat to Andrew's left, Mara took the chair on his right, and Nico claimed the score pad with a flourish that made Darius reach for it.

“I can write numbers,” Nico said.

“Write only the numbers.” Darius let go of the pad. “No commentary.”

Nico looked at the pencil. “A very narrow use of the page.”

Juno had stretched out on the couch, boots on the floor and one arm folded behind his head. Akari sat on the other end with her knees drawn up. Sera brought her mug over and settled in the chair beside them, close enough to see the table.

The first deal took longer than it should have because one card had stuck inside the box. Yelena found it, held it up for Darius to count, and shuffled again.

Andrew sorted his hand while Nico announced that he would be impartial. Mara asked him to move his elbow off her cards. He moved it, then spent the next minute finding a better position for the score pad.

Hearts were trumps. Andrew reminded himself twice and still nearly missed his turn.

Darius led a low club. Mara played over it, Andrew took the trick, and Yelena leaned back to study him with an expression he did not trust. He led again, pleased with the start.

Three tricks later, he put his king of clubs on Darius's ace.

Darius looked at the king. Then he looked at Andrew.

“I had that one,” he said.

Andrew glanced at the low club still in his hand.

From the couch, Juno made a small, pained noise.

“You weren't even looking,” Andrew told him.

“Heard Darius.”

Mara gathered her remaining cards into a neat fan. “You can keep giving him presents. We don't object.”

“I was being supportive,” Andrew said.

Darius laid the won cards face down. “Support me with the small ones, please.”

Akari laughed. Nico started to write something beside the score, saw Darius watching, and erased it before anyone could read it.

The next few tricks went badly enough that Andrew stopped talking. Yelena seemed to have kept every useful heart in the deck. Each time he thought she must have run out, she put down another, unhurried and almost apologetic.

Mara grinned when the last one fell.

“You knew she had that,” Andrew said.

“I hoped.”

“She tapped the table,” Nico said.

Yelena looked at him. “Because you were leaning on it.”

Nico considered the accusation, then moved his chair back a little.

They lost the first hand. Darius accepted the score, collected the cards, and slid the deck to Andrew for the next deal.

“We can recover,” he said.

“How far behind are we?”

“You don't need to look yet.”

Andrew laughed and dealt. His shoulder ached when he reached for the last card, so he changed hands. Yelena noticed; he saw her notice. She said nothing when he left the sore arm resting on the table.

Between hands, Akari asked Nico to put on something quiet. He went to fetch his little speaker and spent a long time choosing a track before Sera suggested the one he had played the evening before. He found it immediately.

The music came through softly enough that they could hear the cards sliding over the table. Juno tapped a finger against his chest in time with it. After a while his hand stopped moving.

Andrew looked up while Mara shuffled.

The new photograph sat a little lower than the old one. From his chair he could see the strip of backing Akari had promised to fix. Mika's bandage was a pale band at the center of the print, impossible to hide now, and Oskar had one hand on the van's unbroken door. Under their feet, Akari's small lettering gave each of them a name.

Leonie had wanted the connection restored. She had it. By now the convoy that had waited for the relay might be well along its route. Andrew had no way to check from this chair, and for the moment he did not need to.

“You're looking at the border,” Akari said.

He turned toward the couch. She had caught him studying it.

“No. You got Oskar to smile.”

“You took it.”

“You told him where to stand.”

Akari looked up at the print herself. “He kept trying to leave a space for you.”

Andrew remembered the driver shuffling sideways while he raised the camera. He hadn't understood why at the time.

“Next one, then,” he said.

“Timer,” Akari replied. “Everyone in.”

Mara finished dealing and nudged Andrew's pile toward him. Darius had already lifted his cards, but he waited while Andrew arranged his own.

Nico turned the score pad so Andrew could finally see it. The numbers were perfectly clear. Beneath their column, in much smaller writing, he had added a drawing of a crown falling into a bin.

“That was a good king,” Andrew said.

“It died very generously,” Nico replied.

Darius held out his hand for the pad. Andrew caught it first and put it beyond both of them.

“Keep score,” he told Nico. “We're keeping the teams.”

Across the table, Darius smiled. Andrew led a small club.
